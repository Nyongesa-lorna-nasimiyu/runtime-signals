# Code of conduct

Runtime Signals is a technical publication and an open source project. We want its issue tracker,
pull requests, reviews, and other community spaces to be useful and safe for everyone who
participates in good faith.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and evidence without attacking the person presenting them.
- Give specific, constructive feedback and accept corrections without hostility.
- Respect differences in experience, identity, language, and technical background.
- Credit sources and other contributors accurately.
- Protect private information shared during security or conduct reports.
- Step away from a discussion when a maintainer asks for a pause.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Discriminatory language or conduct.
- Personal insults, intimidation, or sustained disruption of a discussion.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Impersonation, deliberate misrepresentation, or evading an enforcement action.
- Plagiarism or knowingly submitting false source attribution.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or helps with an investigation.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository activity and to anyone representing Runtime Signals in an
official community space, event, or account.

Disagreement with an editorial or technical decision is not, by itself, a conduct violation.
Keep the discussion focused on the work and use the review or appeal process described below.

## Reporting a concern

Email [care@runtimesignals.tech](mailto:care@runtimesignals.tech). Do not open a public issue for a
conduct report.

Include the relevant links, dates, and a short description of what happened. Share only the
personal information needed to investigate the report. The maintainer will acknowledge a report
within three business days when possible and will keep it private except where disclosure is
needed to protect someone or comply with law.

If the report concerns the person who normally handles reports, say so in the email. That person
will not take part in the investigation or decision.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove content, issue a private warning, limit participation temporarily, or ban a
participant. The response will depend on the severity, impact, and pattern of the behavior. Serious
threats or harassment may result in an immediate ban.

A person affected by an enforcement decision may request one review by replying to the original
notice. A reviewer who was not the subject of the complaint will consider the request when one is
available.
